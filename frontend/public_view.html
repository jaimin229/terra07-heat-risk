What to do right now

Seek shade during afternoon hours
Avoid prolonged exertion between 11:30 AM and 4:30 PM. Rest in shaded or air-conditioned spaces.
Drink water regularly before feeling thirsty
Carry a water bottle. Drink 250–300 ml cool water or lemon/electrolyte water every 20–30 minutes in the sun.
Check on seniors and children
Older adults and infants absorb heat faster and show fewer sweating signals. Ensure they have cooling fans or AC.

Recognizing Heat-Related Illness

Understand symptoms early and take corrective action before conditions worsen.

Heat Exhaustion (Early Warning)
  • Heavy sweating and cold, clammy skin
  • Dizziness, lightheadedness, or fatigue
  • Weakness and muscle cramps
  • Nausea or mild headache
Action: Move to an air-conditioned room or deep shade immediately. Loosen tight clothing. Sip cool water or ORS electrolyte. Rest for at least 30 minutes.
Heatstroke (Medical Emergency)
  • Extremely high body temperature
  • Hot, red, dry skin (sweating has stopped)
  • Confusion, altered mental state, or slurred speech
  • Vomiting, seizures, or loss of consciousness
Action: Call 108 immediately. While waiting, move person to shade, apply cool wet towels or ice packs to neck and armpits. Do not give fluids if unconscious.

Hydration & Daily Tips

Recommended Fluids

Drink plain drinking water, ORS (oral rehydration salts), buttermilk (chaas), lemon water (nimbu pani), or tender coconut water. Avoid excessively sugary sodas or alcohol during daytime.

Clothing & Protection

Wear loose, light-colored, breathable cotton clothing. Use a wide-brimmed hat, umbrella, or cloth towel to shade the head and neck while walking in the open.
