Data & Methodology

How the Heat-Risk Score is calculated under the IPCC AR6 Framework

A. What is Heat Risk?

Heat risk represents the combined effect of heat exposure, physical conditions, and population vulnerability. City-wide temperature forecasts treat large urban regions as uniform, hiding severe microclimate hotspots in crowded industrial and unshaded wards.

B. Mathematical Formulation (IPCC AR6 Tri-Factor Model)

Heat Risk Index (HRI) = 0.40 × Hazard + 0.30 × Exposure + 0.30 × Vulnerability

Where Hazard integrates Landsat Land Surface Temperature (LST) and building density; Exposure captures foot-traffic density and outdoor manual laborers; Vulnerability captures elderly residents (65+), young children (<5), and AC deficiency.

C. Data Sources

  • Satellite Remote Sensing: NASA/USGS Landsat-8/9 TIRS (Band 10) for Land Surface Temperature and Sentinel-2 (B4, B8) for NDVI Vegetation Index.
  • Urban Morphology: OpenStreetMap road network, building footprints, and cooling infrastructure.
  • Demographics: WorldPop 100m spatial resolution population rasters and Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation ward statistics.

D. Limitations & Honest Disclosures

This system is a prototype built for the Agnitia Hackathon. Microclimate and diurnal solar cycle metrics use calibrated physical synthetic estimates where local meteorological stations are sparse. The system supports urban planning and does not replace official emergency services.
